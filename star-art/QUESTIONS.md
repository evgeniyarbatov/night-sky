# Questions

Seeds for wandering. Not a research agenda, not a backlog. Return here when the code feels finished but the feeling is not.

This project turns real coordinates and real catalog data into quiet images. The questions below are what that practice might let you *ask* — of yourself, of the sky, of anyone who stands where you stand. Some can be explored with a new render. Some need only silence. Most have no final answer.

---

## The sky you actually see

- From my window tonight, how many stars are honestly visible — five, twenty, a hundred? What does it mean to render thousands when I would see almost none?
- Is the city sky *broken*, or is it a different kind of sky — one that teaches withholding?
- When almost nothing appears on the page, is that emptiness more honest than a dark-site image full of ink?
- What do I lose when I simulate what I cannot see? What do I gain?
- If I ghost the hidden stars behind the visible few, am I showing truth or longing?
- Does making the invisible visible increase wonder — or does it flatten the mystery I live with every night?
- How long would I have to stand on my rooftop before I saw one more star than I see on the first glance?
- Is light pollution an obstacle to this project, or is it the subject?

---

## Place and where you stand

- Why does the sky from my apartment matter more than the sky from Mauna Kea?
- What is overhead at the bus stop I use every morning — not at dusk for art, but at 6 AM when I never look up?
- If I add my childhood backyard to the locations file, am I mapping stars or mapping memory?
- Does the same zenith look different from two windows on the same street? From two cities I have loved?
- What does it mean that the universe has no opinion about which coordinate is "home"?
- When I render every location in the file at the same instant, am I seeing one sky or many skies on one planet?
- Can a star map be a portrait of a place without showing the ground at all?
- If I moved cities, which stars followed me and which did I leave behind?

---

## Scale and the distance inside numbers

- Proxima Centauri is four light-years away. Can a dot on paper carry that weight, or does the number need to sit beside it like a whisper?
- Betelgeuse and a faint anonymous star are both points of ink. When should they not feel like peers?
- Andromeda is 2.5 million light-years overhead on many nights I never went outside. What does it mean to mark something I could never resolve with my eyes?
- If I draw a path from the nearest star to a quasar, each step a jump in scale — where along that path do I stop understanding?
- Is the universe large in a way that helps me, or only in a way that makes me quiet?
- When I feel small looking at the image, is that humility or dread? Can the same sky produce both?
- Does knowing the distance to Sagittarius A* change the ink, or only the person reading it?
- What is the smallest astronomical fact that, once known, cannot be unknown while standing under the real sky?

---

## Time and what you missed

- The stars were in that exact arrangement on the night I was born. Was anyone looking? Was I?
- If I render astronomical dusk on a date that mattered — a wedding, a funeral, the night I left — am I recovering something or inventing a witness?
- How much of a human life fits inside one rotation of the field? One timelapse night?
- The sky I see at solstice and the sky at equinox: what stays, what drifts, what only the catalog knows changed?
- Precession will erase our constellations in twenty thousand years. Should I mourn that, or find it funny?
- If I render the sky from my window in 12,000 BCE, who is the audience — cave painters, or me?
- When I generate a month of daily zenith images, am I documenting sameness or watching slow transformation?
- What night sky did my grandparents see from this latitude? My grandchildren?
- Is astronomical dusk the right moment for wonder, or should I render the hour I actually step outside?

---

## Names, patterns, and making meaning

- Who decided Orion looks like a hunter, and why do I still see him?
- When only six named stars are visible from the city and I connect them with a path — is that a constellation or a confession of poverty?
- What if I draw Cassiopeia's W but only mark stars *below* the visibility threshold — an outline with no members inside?
- Is a shortest path between bright stars astronomy or aesthetics? What if the path followed a myth instead of an algorithm?
- Do star names in Arabic, Latin, Chinese, and Dakota all point at the same object, or at different relationships to it?
- When I label Sirius, am I naming a ball of plasma or naming something human — a season, a direction, a story?
- Can a sparse wabi-sabi path be a poem? Who is it for?
- If constellations are temporary human graffiti on eternal objects, what does that say about every map I make?

---

## The invisible majority

- Galaxies and quasars are already in the codebase. From my city they are invisible by definition. Why render them anyway?
- What is overhead that no human eye on Earth has ever resolved without glass?
- If I make a gallery wall of only deep objects — black holes, pulsars, distant galaxies — from a light-polluted coordinate, is it a map of the sky or a map of human reach?
- Does marking the unseeable respect the universe's scale, or is it a kind of bragging?
- How many points on the page represent civilizations, collisions, and deaths I will never comprehend?
- Is there comfort in knowing Andromeda is up there even when I cannot see it, or only a sharper sense of absence?

---

## Looking up vs. making an image

- Does generating the sky count as stargazing, or is it a substitute I should distrust?
- When I finally stand under a dark sky in person, will these images still matter — or become obsolete?
- Is accuracy a form of respect for the real objects, or is poetic license allowed when it serves the feeling?
- Should the footer metadata stay — coordinates, magnitude, time — or is silence better for something hung on a wall?
- What changes when the medium is sumi ink on pale paper instead of photons on the retina?
- Is the stereographic projection a lie that tells a truth, or a truth that hides another?
- If I looked at the stars from the side — a shell, not a ceiling — would I feel inside the universe differently?
- Does the timelapse teach me motion I never noticed, or replace the patience of a whole night outside?

---

## The body under the dome

- I live inside a rotating shell of distant fires. Why does that fact rarely reach my body on a Tuesday evening?
- What does it mean that most of what I render is older than every human institution?
- Am I in the universe, or is the universe in what I can see from this latitude?
- Why do we look up when we want awe and rarely when we want practical answers?
- Is feeling small a skill that can be practiced from a city balcony?
- What would change if everyone on my street knew what was directly overhead at midnight?
- Does the night sky belong to everyone equally, or does access to darkness divide us?

---

## Seasons of a life with this project

- What happens if I keep one location, one set of parameters, and render it once a month for a year — then five years?
- Will the images become a calendar of my attention, or proof that I looked away more than I looked up?
- At what point does a series stop being an experiment and become a diary?
- If I print a magnitude ladder — mag 2 through mag 12 in a row — and hang it where I eat breakfast, will I live differently?
- What is the right stopping point for a long-running personal project about infinity?
- When a direction in the roadmap stops pulling me, is that failure or maturity?

---

## Gifts, witnesses, and other people

- If I mail a postcard of a quasar to someone I love, what am I sending — beauty, scale, or proof I was thinking of them under the same sky?
- Can a star map be a love letter without words?
- Who would understand a diptych of city sky beside dark-site sky without explanation?
- If I render the sky on the night someone was born and give it to them, is it personal or cosmic?
- Do these images ask for an audience, or are they complete when only I have seen them?

---

## Questions the code may grow into

These are not yet images. They are invitations for when the tools catch up to the wondering.

- What did the sky look like the hour before I knew something terrible? The hour after?
- Can two people's meaningful nights — different cities, same UTC instant — be composed as one piece?
- What if the horizon slice above my actual roofline became the only view I ever rendered?
- Could a flipbook of one night make a child feel the Earth's turn in their hands?
- What would a zine of invisible objects read like — page after page of things that were there while I slept?
- If I stack twelve magnitude layers with fading opacity, do I see time, patience, or imagination?
- What is the sound of this project? (Maybe nothing. Maybe that is the answer.)

---

## Questions to leave permanently open

- When you finally see a dark sky in person, will the images still matter?
- Is wonder a renewable resource or something numbed by too much rendering?
- Does showing more stars increase awe, or does emptiness do more work from the city?
- What is this project for, if not for an audience, a product, or a use case?
- After months of making these images, will I look up more — or only look down at the next render?

Do not rush to answer these. Let a question sit until it pairs with a coordinate, a date, or a night when you are actually awake.
